# Mansha origin check: aluminium window frames

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

Your aluminium window frames qualify for the reduced tariff rate under the India-UAE CEPA. Claiming this rate can save an estimated 10000.00 AED in import duty on your shipment. Please refer to the checklist below for the required steps to claim this preference.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff code 76101000 for doors, windows and their frames and thresholds for doors (aluminium), with high confidence. You should confirm this code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate of 10% is reduced to a CEPA rate of 0%. This provides an estimated duty saving of 10000.00 AED on your shipment. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The product must meet a Change in Tariff Subheading (CTSH)—where each non-originating material is classified in a different 6-digit subheading than the final product—and achieve a Value Addition (VA) of 45% based on the FOB price at the UAE port. The product qualifies because final manufacture took place in the UAE and operations go beyond minimal operations. All 3 non-originating inputs meet the CTSH requirement by being classified outside subheading 761010, and the build-down value addition reaches 49.0%, exceeding the 45% threshold. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] The Ministry of Economy and Tourism requires the factory to complete Factory Registration prior to certificate application, requiring a location outside a free zone, a MoIAT industrial licence, and a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added, taking about 3 working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade provides the Certificate of Origin for India e-service on moet.gov.ae, processed in about one working day for registered manufacturers upon uploading proof of factory registration and commercial invoice.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade charges a certificate fee of AED 50 for an invoice value up to AED 100000.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The competent authority issues the certificate of origin prior to, at, or within five (5) working days of the date of exportation, valid for 12 months for a single import.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [10][11]
- [ ] The exporter must ensure the product is shipped directly from the UAE to India and indicate third-party invoicing if an invoice is issued by a third party.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry, holds origin information under CAROTAR 2020 for at least 5 years, and reportedly uploads electronic certificates to e-Sanchit (unconfirmed).  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter must keep origin records, including cost breakdowns, for no less than five (5) years starting from the end of the year of issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]

## Important notes

- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][9][10][11][15][16]
- Duty savings are estimated on the shipment value given; India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. [2][3]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only; IGST, GST compensation cess, Social Welfare Surcharge, and any anti-dumping or safeguard duties still apply. [2][3]
- The '+' in a rule is read as 'and' (both tests must pass), which is Mansha's interpretation as the agreement does not define '+'. [4]
- Operations and final manufacture rely on the exporter's own declaration. [4]
