## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] The Ministry of Economy and Tourism registers the factory (the exporter must apply before requesting a certificate).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] The exporter completes the application with the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [7]
- [ ] The exporter uploads proof of factory registration, the commercial invoice, and, if a trading exporter, the factory's purchase invoice.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][8][9][10]
- [ ] The exporter pays the certificate fee online (fee range AED 10 to AED 200).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][8][9][10]
- [ ] The exporter submits the application through the Ministry of Foreign Trade e-service.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][8][9][10]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade processes the application (about one working day) and issues the certificate, or returns it for correction.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][8][9][10]
- [ ] The certificate must be issued before, at, or within five working days of export, is valid for twelve months from issue, and covers only one import.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [7][11]
- [ ] The exporter ships the goods directly from the UAE to India; if transit occurs, it must be under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading or storage.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][11]
- [ ] The exporter indicates third-party invoicing if applicable.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and holds origin information, including which origin criterion is claimed.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The Indian importer keeps supporting documents for at least five years from filing and answers customs requests within ten working days.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter keeps origin records, including cost breakdown, for at least five years from the end of the calendar year of issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][11]

## Important notes

- The CEPA rate reduces Basic Customs Duty only; IGST, GST compensation cess, Social Welfare Surcharge and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- '+' in the rule is read as 'and' (both tests must pass); the agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [4][7][11]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][8][10][11][15][16]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, which is required before applying for the certificate; confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- Electronic certificates are reportedly accepted and uploaded to India's e-Sanchit system, but this is seen only in secondary sources, not the official text. [12][13][14]
- When completing the certificate of origin, use box 9 for remarks, as India's form governs entry into India. [7][11]
- The issuing authority may apply risk management, which could include requesting detailed cost sheets and visiting the factory. [7]
