# Mansha origin check: aluminium window frames

**Result: Cannot confirm yet: more information needed**

## Summary

Mansha cannot confirm whether the aluminium window frames qualify for the preferential duty rate under the India‑UAE CEPA because required information is missing. To estimate the duty saving, the shipment value and a complete bill of materials plus a declaration are needed.

## What's still needed

- Provide the shipment value in AED (or another currency) so the duty saving can be estimated.
- Provide a bill of materials with each input's description, 6-digit HS code, origin country, value and currency, plus a declaration stating the product price, whether it is on an FOB or Ex-Works basis, that final manufacture occurred in the UAE, and what operations were done in the UAE.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 76101000, described as doors, windows and their frames and thresholds for doors (aluminium). Mansha’s confidence in this classification is high, but you should verify it with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| tariff 10%, effective 10% | 0% | cannot be estimated without the shipment value |

Under the India‑UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate is 10% and the CEPA rate is 0% for Basic Customs Duty. Because the shipment value is not provided, the saving cannot be estimated. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH) and at least 45% value addition (VA). CTSH means each non‑originating input must be classified in a different 6‑digit subheading than the product, and VA measures the share of the product’s price that does not come from non‑originating materials. No origin checks could be performed because the necessary data, including a bill of materials and a declaration, are missing. Therefore the product cannot yet be assessed for origin. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] The Ministry of Economy and Tourism registers the factory through its Factory Registration service. The factory must be located outside a free zone, hold a MoIAT industrial licence, and provide a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added. Registration takes about 3 working days. Traders can apply only for goods bought from a registered factory.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade provides the Certificate of Origin for India e-service. The applicant logs in to the ministry e-services portal, chooses the Certificate of Origin for India service, and fills in the application form.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The issuing authority requires the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product. Each item on the certificate must qualify separately in its own right. The issuing authority may apply risk management, including asking for detailed cost sheets and conducting a factory visit.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [10]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade requires proof of factory registration, the commercial invoice, and, for a trading exporter, the factory's purchase invoice.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade charges a certificate fee of AED 10 to AED 200, depending on the invoice value in AED. No usable shipment value was provided, so only the fee range is available.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade processes the application in about one working day; the service runs 24/7. The applicant receives approval, or a return with comments to correct.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The competent authority issues the Certificate of Origin prior to, at, or within five working days of the date of exportation. The certificate is valid for twelve months from the date of issue and for only one import; it may list several products. It must bear the authorised signature and official seal of the competent authority. A certificate may be issued retrospectively up to 12 months from the date of shipment, marked 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9 (Remarks) of India's certificate form.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [10][11]
- [ ] The exporter ensures the goods are shipped directly from the UAE to India. Transit through another country is allowed only under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading, storage or similar. If a third party issues the invoice, the exporter must indicate 'third-party invoicing'.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and holds origin information under CAROTAR 2020, including which origin criterion is claimed. The importer keeps supporting documents for at least five years from filing and must answer a customs request within ten working days. Electronic certificates are reportedly accepted and uploaded to e-Sanchit, though this is unconfirmed.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter keeps origin records, including the cost breakdown, for no less than five years starting from the end of the year of the date of issue. Any failure to consent to a verification visit is grounds for denial of preferential benefits. India may ask the UAE authority to verify a certificate; the authority answers an origin verification request within ninety days, extendable by sixty.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]

## Important notes

- The '+' in the rule is interpreted as 'and' (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- No usable shipment value (positive amount and currency) was provided, so only rates and percentage-point differences are given; the actual duty saving cannot be computed. [2][3]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [4][10][11]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][9][10][11][15][16]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- The CEPA rate reduces only Basic Customs Duty; IGST, GST compensation cess, Social Welfare Surcharge and any anti‑dumping or safeguard duty still apply. Because IGST is calculated on the sum of BCD and other charges, a lower BCD also reduces the IGST amount. [14][17]
- Activities listed in Article 3.5.1 (a) to (q), alone or combined, never make a product originating, regardless of the product‑specific rule. 'Simple' means the activity requires no special skills and no specially made or installed machines or equipment. [10][11]
