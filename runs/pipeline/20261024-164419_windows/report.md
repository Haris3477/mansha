# Mansha origin check: aluminium window frames

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

Aluminium window frames qualify for the preferential rate under the India-UAE CEPA because the product meets the rule of origin. Claiming this preference can save an estimated 10000.00 AED in import duty in India. Follow the claim checklist below to complete the process.

## Product classification

The product is classified under Indian tariff code 76101000 for doors, windows and their frames and thresholds for doors (aluminium), with high confidence. You should confirm this tariff code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal import duty (MFN rate) of 10% is reduced to a CEPA rate of 0%. On a shipment value of 100000 AED, this provides an estimated saving of 10000.00 AED. The CEPA rate applies to Basic Customs Duty, while other import charges such as IGST remain payable. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

To qualify, the product must satisfy a change in tariff subheading (CTSH), meaning every non-originating input must be classified in a different 6-digit subheading than the final product, and achieve a value addition (VA) of at least 45% on a free on board (FOB) price basis. The product meets this rule because final manufacturing occurred in the UAE, the manufacturing processes go beyond simple minimal operations, all 3 non-originating inputs are classified outside subheading 761010, and the value addition reached 49.0%. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] Register the factory with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism through the Factory Registration service; requires factory outside a free zone, a MoIAT industrial licence and a letter confirming 40% domestic value added; processing takes about 3 working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade issues the preferential certificate of origin via its e-service.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9][10][11][12]
- [ ] Log in to the ministry e-services portal, choose the Certificate of Origin for India service, fill in the application form, upload proof of factory registration and the commercial invoice (and the factory’s purchase invoice if a trading exporter), pay the fee online and submit; processing takes about one working day.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][13]
- [ ] Pay the certificate fee of AED 50 (invoice value up to AED 100000).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][13]
- [ ] The certificate must be issued before, at or within five working days of the date of exportation; it can be issued retrospectively up to 12 months from shipment, marked ‘ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY’ in box 9 of India’s certificate form.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [9][10]
- [ ] The certificate is valid for twelve months from the date of issue and covers only one import (it may list several products).  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [9][10]
- [ ] The goods must be shipped directly from the UAE to India; transit through another country is allowed only under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading or storage. If a third party issues the invoice, the exporter must indicate ‘third-party invoicing’.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [9][10]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and must hold origin information, including which origin criterion is claimed; they keep supporting documents for at least five years and must answer a customs request within ten working days.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [14][15][16]
- [ ] The exporter must keep origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least five years starting from the end of the calendar year of the certificate’s issue; refusal to allow a verification visit is grounds for denying preferential benefits.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [9][10]

## Important notes

- Estimate on the shipment value given. India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. Shown in the shipment’s own currency; never converted. [2][3]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, SWS and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- ‘+’ in a rule is read as ‘and’ (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define ‘+’, so this is Mansha’s interpretation. [4]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter’s own declaration. [4]
- ‘Direct labour cost’ and ‘direct overhead cost’ are not defined in the agreement. [4][9][10]
- Interpretation: de minimis helps only the tariff-shift part of a rule, not the VA percentage. [9][10]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][8][9][10][11][12]
- Factory registration requires the factory to be located outside a free zone, a MoIAT industrial licence, and a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added. A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible. [5][6]
- Electronic certificates are reportedly accepted and uploaded to e-Sanchit (unconfirmed: seen only in secondary sources). [14][15][16]
- Goods must be shipped directly from the UAE to India, though transit through a third country is permitted under customs control if no operations other than unloading, reloading, or storage take place. [9][10]
- The exporter must keep all origin documentation and cost breakdowns for at least five years from the end of the calendar year the certificate is issued. [9][10]
