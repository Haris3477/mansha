# Mansha origin check: battery chargers

**Result: Not eligible: the normal (MFN) rate applies**

## Summary

The product does not meet the rule of origin, so the normal (MFN) rate applies. The checks that failed are listed under the rule of origin below.

## Product classification

Tariff line 85044030: Battery chargers (Mansha's confidence: high). Confirm the code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3][4]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| 20% | 0% | none, because the product does not qualify |

Agreement: India-UAE CEPA. Estimated saving: none, because the product does not qualify. [1][2][3][4]

## Rule of origin and result

Rule of origin: CTH + VA 40%. Result: the product does not meet the rule of origin. Final manufacture did not take place in the UAE. The operations performed are all minimal operations under Article 3.5.1 (changes of packaging, simple packaging, affixing marks). One non-originating input is in the same heading 8504 as the product, representing 88.0% of the price, above the 10% de minimis. Value addition is only 12.0% against a required 40% on an FOB basis. [5]

## Important notes

- The '+' in the rule is interpreted as 'and' (both tests must pass); the agreement does not define '+'. [5]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [5]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically; the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][8][9][10][11][12]
- A lower 15% rate under Notification 45/2025-Customs applies only for electric-vehicle manufacture; Mansha uses the general 20% rate. [1][2][3][4]
- Mansha has not checked whether India charges anti-dumping duty on this product; check with Indian customs or a customs broker. [1][2][3][4]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, which is required before applying for the certificate of origin for India. [8][13]
- Electronic certificates are reportedly accepted and uploaded to India's e-Sanchit system, but this is unconfirmed. [14][15][16]

## What's still needed

- Mansha has not checked whether India charges anti-dumping duty on battery chargers.
