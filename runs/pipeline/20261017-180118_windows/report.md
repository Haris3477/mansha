# Mansha origin check: aluminium window frames

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The aluminium window frames meet the rule of origin, so the CEPA rate can be claimed. This results in an estimated saving of 10000.00 AED. See the checklist below for the steps to claim the preference.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 76101000, Doors, windows and their frames and thresholds for doors (aluminium). Mansha's confidence in this classification is high, but you should confirm the code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate is 10% and the CEPA rate is 0%. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH) and a value addition (VA) of at least 45%. CTSH means each non‑originating input must be classified in a different 6‑digit subheading than the product, while VA is the share of the product's price that does not come from non‑originating materials, calculated on an FOB basis. The final manufacture took place in the UAE, the work goes beyond the minimal operations, all three non‑originating inputs satisfy the tariff shift, and the value addition of 49.0% exceeds the 45% threshold. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] The Ministry of Economy and Tourism requires factory registration before a certificate of origin can be applied for; registration needs the factory to be outside a free zone, a MoIAT industrial licence and a letter confirming 40% domestic value added, and takes about 3 working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade provides the e-service: log in to the ministry e-services portal, choose the Certificate of Origin for India service, fill in the application form, upload proof of factory registration and the commercial invoice (plus the factory's purchase invoice if a trading exporter), pay the fee online and submit; processing takes about one working day.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade charges a certificate fee of AED 50 for an invoice value up to AED 100000.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The competent authority issues the certificate: it is valid for 12 months from issue, covers one import, and should be issued before, at or within 5 working days of export; it can be issued retrospectively up to 12 months from shipment, marked 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9 of India's form.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [10][11]
- [ ] The exporter ships the goods directly from the UAE to India; transit through another country is allowed only under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading or storage. If a third party issues the invoice, the exporter must indicate 'third-party invoicing'.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry, holds origin information including which criterion is claimed, keeps supporting documents for at least 5 years from filing and answers customs requests within 10 working days.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter keeps origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least 5 years starting from the end of the calendar year of the certificate's issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]

## Important notes

- The '+' in the rule 'CTSH + VA 45%' is read as 'and' (both tests must pass); the agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- The final manufacture and the operations described are the exporter's own declaration. [4]
- India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ from the estimate shown. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate reduces Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only; IGST, GST compensation cess, SWS and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][9][10][11][15][16]
- If the producer is located in a free zone, it is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, which is required before applying for the certificate of origin for India. The 40% value‑added letter is a Factory Registration condition only and is not the CEPA product‑specific rule test. [5][6]
