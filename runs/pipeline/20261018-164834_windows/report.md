# Mansha origin check: aluminium window frames

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The aluminium window frames meet the rule of origin under the India-UAE CEPA, so the CEPA rate of 0% can be claimed, resulting in an estimated saving of 10000.00 AED. Follow the checklist below to complete the claim.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 76101000, Doors, windows and their frames and thresholds for doors (aluminium). Mansha's confidence in this classification is high, but the code should be confirmed with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal MFN rate is 10% and the CEPA rate is 0%, giving an estimated saving of 10000.00 AED. The CEPA rate applies only to Basic Customs Duty, so other import charges such as IGST remain payable. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH) for each non-originating input, meaning each such input must be classified in a different six‑digit subheading than the product, and a value addition (VA) of at least 45%, which is the share of the product's price that does not come from non‑originating materials. The final manufacture took place in the UAE, and the manufacturing operations go beyond the minimal operations that would not confer origin on their own. The CTSH requirement is met because all three non‑originating inputs are classified outside subheading 761010, and the value addition requirement is met with a build‑down calculation of 49.0% on an FOB basis, above the 45% threshold. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] The Ministry of Economy and Tourism requires the factory to be registered through its Factory Registration service prior to applying for a certificate of origin, requiring the factory to be outside a free zone, hold a MoIAT industrial licence, and have a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added, which takes about 3 working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] The issuing authority requires the application to include the HS code, description, quantity, and value of the exported product, and may apply risk management such as requesting detailed cost sheets and conducting a factory visit.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [7]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade provides the 'Certificate of Origin for India' e-service where the applicant logs into the portal, selects the service, fills in the form, uploads proof of factory registration and commercial invoice, pays online, and receives processing in about one working day.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][8][9][10]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade charges a certificate fee of AED 50 for the invoice value band up to AED 100000.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][8][9][10]
- [ ] The competent authority must issue the certificate of origin prior to, at, or within five (5) working days of the date of exportation, bearing an authorised signature and official seal, valid for twelve (12) months from issue for a single import.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [7][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry, must hold origin information under CAROTAR 2020 including the origin criterion claimed, must keep supporting documents for at least 5 years from filing, and must answer customs inquiries within 10 working days; electronic certificates are reportedly accepted and uploaded to e-Sanchit (unconfirmed).  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter must keep origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least 5 years starting from the end of the year of the date of issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][11]

## Important notes

- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][8][10][11][15][16]
- '+' in a rule is read as 'and' (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- Operations and final manufacture are based on the exporter's own declaration. [4]
- Estimate on the shipment value given. India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. Shown in the shipment's own currency; never converted. [2][3]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, SWS and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
