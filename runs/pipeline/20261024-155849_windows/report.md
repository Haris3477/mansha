# Mansha origin check: aluminium window frames

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

Your product, aluminium window frames, meets the rule of origin under the India-UAE CEPA, so you can claim the preferential CEPA rate of 0% instead of the normal rate. This results in an estimated saving of 10000.00 AED for this shipment. Please refer to the claim checklist below to complete the necessary steps for claiming this preference.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 76101000, which covers doors, windows and their frames and thresholds for doors (aluminium). Mansha's confidence in this classification is high, but you should confirm the code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| tariff 10%, effective 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate for this product is 10%, while the preferential CEPA rate is 0%. Since the product qualifies, you can claim the CEPA rate of 0%, which results in an estimated saving of 10000.00 AED for this shipment. Note that the CEPA rate applies to Basic Customs Duty only, so other import charges such as IGST are still payable. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

To qualify for the CEPA rate, the product must meet the rule of origin, which requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH) and at least 45% value addition (VA). CTSH means each non-originating input must be classified in a different 6-digit subheading than the product, and VA is the share of the product's price that does not come from non-originating materials. The product meets this rule because the final manufacture check passed, meaning the last manufacturing step took place in the UAE, and the minimal operations check passed, meaning the UAE work goes beyond simple operations that do not grant origin on their own. Additionally, the CTSH check passed as all 3 non-originating inputs are classified outside subheading 761010, and the VA check passed with a 49.0% value addition against the 45% threshold, calculated on an FOB basis (the price of the goods loaded on board at the UAE port). [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] The Ministry of Economy and Tourism registers the factory (a prerequisite for the certificate).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] The exporter ensures that the goods are shipped directly from the UAE to India, or if in transit, under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading, or storage.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8]
- [ ] If the invoice is issued by a third party, the exporter must indicate 'third-party invoicing'.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade provides an e-service through which the exporter applies for a Certificate of Origin for India by logging in, choosing the service, filling the form, uploading the required documents, paying the fee online, and submitting.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][9][10][11]
- [ ] The issuing authority requires the application to include the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product, with each item qualifying separately.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [7]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade charges a certificate fee of AED 50 for an invoice value up to AED 100,000.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][9][10][11]
- [ ] The competent authority issues the certificate before, at, or within 5 working days of export.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [7][8]
- [ ] The certificate is valid for 12 months from issue and covers one import.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [7][8]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and holds origin information.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter keeps origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least 5 years from the end of the calendar year of issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8]

## Important notes

- India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. [2][3]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, SWS and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- '+' in a rule is read as 'and' (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [4]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [4][7][8]
- Inference: SWS is charged on the reduced BCD (no CEPA-specific text found). [14][15]
- Inference: Anti-dumping and safeguard duties still apply (no exemption found). [14][15]
- Electronic certificates are reportedly accepted and uploaded to India's e-Sanchit system, but this is unconfirmed and seen only in secondary sources. [12][13][14]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][8][9][11][16][17]
- The trade agreement does not clearly state whether the build-down and build-up methods use the same value addition threshold, except for a 5-point Ex-Works adjustment. [4][7][8]
- While the agreement refers to box 8, India's origin rules and certificate form require box 9, and India's form governs entry into the country. [7][8]
