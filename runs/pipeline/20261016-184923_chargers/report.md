# Mansha origin check: battery chargers

**Result: Not eligible: the normal (MFN) rate applies**

## Summary

The product does not meet the rule of origin, so the normal (MFN) rate applies. The main failed check is that final manufacture did not take place in the UAE.

## Product classification

The tariff line is 85044030, Battery chargers, and Mansha's confidence is high. The code should be confirmed with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3][4]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| tariff 20%, effective 20% | 0% (2026 CEPA rate) | none, because the product does not qualify |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate is 20% and the CEPA rate is 0% (2026 CEPA rate). No saving applies because the product does not qualify. [1][2][3][4]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff heading (CTH), meaning each non-originating input must be classified in a different 4-digit heading than the product, and a value addition (VA) of at least 40% of the product's FOB price, where FOB is the price loaded on board at the UAE port. The final manufacture check failed because the last manufacturing step did not take place in the UAE, and the minimal operations check failed because the work done, repacking and relabeling, is considered minimal operations that never confer origin. The CTH test failed because a non-originating input falls in the same heading 8504, and the VA test failed because the value addition is only 12.0%, below the required 40%. [5]

## Important notes

- The operations and final manufacture results are based on the exporter's own declaration. [5]
- A lower 15% rate under India's Notification 45/2025-Customs applies only for electric-vehicle manufacture; the general 20% rate was used. [1][2][3][4]
- Whether India charges anti-dumping duty on battery chargers has not been checked; confirm with Indian customs or a customs broker. [1][2][3][4]
- The '+' in the rule 'CTH + VA 40%' is read as 'and' so both tests must pass; the agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [5]
