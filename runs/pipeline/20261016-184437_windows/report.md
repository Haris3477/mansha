# Mansha origin check: aluminium window frames

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The product meets the rule of origin, so the CEPA rate can be claimed. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. See the checklist below for the required steps.

## Product classification

The tariff line is 76101000, Doors, windows and their frames and thresholds for doors (aluminium), and Mansha's confidence is high. Please confirm this code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate is 10% and the CEPA rate is 0%. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH) for each non-originating input and a value addition (VA) of at least 45% on an FOB basis. The final manufacture and minimal operations checks both passed, meaning the last manufacturing step took place in the UAE and the work went beyond the simple operations that would not give origin on their own. All three non-originating inputs are classified outside subheading 761010, satisfying the CTSH test, and the value addition by build-down is 49.0%, which exceeds the 45% threshold, so the product meets the rule of origin. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] The Ministry of Economy and Tourism registers the factory through its Factory Registration service before applying for a certificate; registration takes about 3 working days and requires the factory to be located outside a free zone, a MoIAT industrial licence, and a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] The issuing authority requires the certificate application to include the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product; each item on the certificate must qualify separately in its own right, and the authority may apply risk management including requesting detailed cost sheets and conducting a factory visit.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [7]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade provides the Certificate of Origin for India e-service; the applicant logs in to the ministry e-services portal, chooses the Certificate of Origin for India service, fills in the application form, uploads the required documents, submits the application, and receives approval or a return with comments to correct, then prints the approved certificate; processing takes about one working day and the service runs 24/7.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][8][9][10]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade charges a certificate fee of AED 50 for an invoice value up to AED 100,000; the fee range of the service is AED 10 to AED 200.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][8][9][10]
- [ ] The competent authority issues the certificate prior to, at, or within five working days of the date of exportation; the certificate is valid for twelve months from the date of issue and covers only one import, and it may be issued retrospectively up to twelve months from the date of shipment, marked 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9 of India's certificate form.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [7][11]
- [ ] The exporter ships the goods directly from the UAE to India; transit through another country is allowed only under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading, storage or similar, and for third-party invoicing the exporter must indicate 'third-party invoicing'.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and must hold origin information under CAROTAR 2020, including which origin criterion is claimed; the importer keeps supporting documents for at least five years from filing and must answer a customs request within ten working days.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter keeps origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least five years starting from the end of the calendar year of the certificate's issue; any failure to consent to a verification visit is grounds for a denial of preferential benefits.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][11]

## Important notes

- India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so the actual duty amounts will differ from the estimate shown. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate reduces Basic Customs Duty only; IGST, GST compensation cess, the Social Welfare Surcharge and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- The '+' in the rule 'CTSH + VA 45%' is read as 'and' (both tests must pass); the agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- The final manufacture in the UAE and the operations beyond minimal operations are the exporter's own declaration. [4]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically; its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae (Ministry of Economy and Tourism). [6][7][8][10][11][15][16]
