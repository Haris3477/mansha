## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH), meaning each non-originating input must be classified in a different 6-digit subheading than the final product, and a value addition (VA) of at least 45%, meaning at least 45% of the product's price must not come from non-originating materials. Because the exporter has not provided a bill of materials and a declaration, the CTSH and VA checks cannot be performed. Therefore the origin result is still pending. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] Register the factory through the Ministry of Economy and Tourism's Factory Registration service; the factory must be outside a free zone, hold a MoIAT industrial licence, and have a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added. Registration takes about 3 working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] Log in to the Ministry of Foreign Trade's e-services portal.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Choose the Certificate of Origin for India service.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Fill in the application form with the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Upload the required documents: proof of factory registration, the commercial invoice, and, for a trading exporter, the factory's purchase invoice.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Pay the certificate fee online; the fee depends on the invoice value in AED and ranges from AED 10 to AED 200.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Submit the application.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Receive approval, or a return with comments to correct.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Print the approved certificate.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry, holds origin information under CAROTAR 2020, keeps supporting documents for at least 5 years from filing, and answers any customs request within 10 working days.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [10][11][12]
- [ ] The exporter must keep origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least 5 years starting from the end of the calendar year of the certificate's issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [13][14]

## Important notes

- Mansha interprets '+' in the rule as 'and' (both tests must pass); the agreement does not define '+'. [4]
- The certificate of origin must be presented for the CEPA rate to apply. [2][3]
- The Ministry of Foreign Trade offers the e-service that issues preferential and non-preferential certificates of origin for locally made products, but no official page read names it as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically; the India-specific page is still on the Ministry of Economy and Tourism website. [6][7][9][13][14][15][16]
- The CEPA rate applies only to Basic Customs Duty; other charges such as IGST, GST compensation cess, Social Welfare Surcharge, and any anti-dumping or safeguard duties remain. [12][17]
- Producers in a free zone may not be eligible for Factory Registration, which is required before applying for the certificate; confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- The certificate is valid for 12 months from issue and covers one import; it should be issued before, at, or within 5 working days of export. It can be issued retrospectively up to 12 months after shipment, marked 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9. [13][14]
