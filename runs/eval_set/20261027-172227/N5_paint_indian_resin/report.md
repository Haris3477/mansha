# Mansha origin check: acrylic paint

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The product meets the rule of origin, so the CEPA rate can be claimed. Claiming this rate saves an estimated 10000.00 AED on the duty for this shipment. Caution: this result counts your inputs from India as originating, which holds only with proof of their originating status; without that proof they count as non-originating inputs. Keep that proof before relying on this result.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 32091010, which is described as acrylic emulsion (paint). Mansha's confidence in this classification is high, but you should confirm the code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| tariff 10%, effective 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate for this tariff line is 10%, while the CEPA rate is 0%. By claiming the CEPA rate, the estimated saving on this shipment is 10000.00 AED. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH), where all non-originating inputs must be classified outside subheading 320910, plus value addition (VA) of at least 40% on an FOB basis. The final manufacture check passed because the last manufacturing step took place in the UAE. The minimal operations check passed, meaning the UAE work goes beyond the simple operations that never give origin on their own. The CTSH check passed as all 2 non-originating inputs were classified outside the target subheading. The VA check passed with a build-down of 65.0% against the 40% threshold. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] The Ministry of Economy and Tourism registers the factory before applying for a certificate. Registration requires the factory to be located outside a free zone, a MoIAT industrial licence, and a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added, and takes about 3 working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade operates the e-service 'Certificate of Origin for India'. The exporter logs in to the ministry e-services portal and chooses this service.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The exporter fills in the application form with the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product. Each item must qualify separately in its own right.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [10]
- [ ] The exporter uploads proof of factory registration, the commercial invoice, and, for a trading exporter, the factory's purchase invoice.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The exporter pays the certificate fee online. The fee is AED 50 for an invoice value up to AED 100000 (band: AED 10 to AED 200).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The exporter submits the application and receives approval, or a return with comments to correct.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The exporter prints the approved certificate.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The competent authority issues the certificate before, at, or within five working days of export. It is valid for twelve months from issue and covers only one import.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [10][11]
- [ ] The issuing authority may apply risk management, including asking for detailed cost sheets and conducting a factory visit.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [10]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and must hold origin information, including which origin criterion is claimed. The importer keeps supporting documents for at least five years from filing and must answer a customs request within ten working days.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter must keep origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least five years starting from the end of the calendar year of the certificate's issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]

## Important notes

- Estimate on the shipment value given. India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. Shown in the shipment's own currency; never converted. [2][3]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, SWS and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [4][10][11]
- '+' in a rule is read as 'and' (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- Indian inputs count as originating only with proof of their originating status (bilateral cumulation, Art. 3.7). [4]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [4]
- Interpretation: de minimis helps only the tariff-shift part of a rule, not the VA percentage. [10][11]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][9][10][11][15][16]
- Inference: a producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- The CEPA rate of 0% reduces the Basic Customs Duty (BCD) and the Agriculture Infrastructure and Development Cess only for lines in Table II. Other charges such as IGST, GST compensation cess, and the Social Welfare Surcharge still apply, though a lower BCD also lowers the IGST amount. [14][17]
- Preference is given only to originating goods shipped directly from the UAE to India. Transit through another country is allowed only under customs control, with no operations other than unloading, reloading, storage or similar. [10][11]
