## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH) for each non-originating input, meaning each such input must be classified in a different 6-digit subheading than the finished product, and a value addition (VA) of at least 40%, which is the share of the product's price that does not come from non-originating materials.   *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade provides the Certificate of Origin for India e-service, which requires the exporter to log in to the ministry e-services portal, choose the service, fill in the application form, upload the required documents, and submit the application.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade charges a certificate fee of AED 50 for an invoice value up to AED 100000.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The competent authority issues the Certificate of Origin prior to, at, or within five working days of export, and it is valid for twelve months from the date of issue for one import.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [10][11]
- [ ] The exporter must ensure the goods are shipped directly from the UAE to India, with transit allowed only under customs control and without operations other than unloading, reloading, or storage.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and must hold origin information, including which origin criterion is claimed.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]

## Important notes

- The '+' in the rule 'CTSH + VA 40%' is interpreted as 'and' (both tests must pass), but the agreement does not define '+'; this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- The duty estimate is on the shipment value given; India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate reduces Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only; IGST, GST compensation cess, Social Welfare Surcharge, and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- Direct labour cost and direct overhead cost are not defined in the agreement. [4][10][11]
- Interpretation: de minimis helps only the tariff-shift part of a rule, not the VA percentage. [10][11]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][9][10][11][15][16]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- Electronic certificates are reportedly accepted and uploaded to India's e-Sanchit system, but this comes from secondary sources (CBIC Instruction 28/2022), not the official text. [12][13][14]
- The agreement refers to box 8 for the certificate remark, but India's origin rules and certificate form use box 9; since India's form governs entry into India, you should use box 9. [10][11]
- UAE law requires records to be kept for at least 3 years, but the CEPA requires 5 years for CEPA claims, so you must keep records for 5 years. [10][11]
