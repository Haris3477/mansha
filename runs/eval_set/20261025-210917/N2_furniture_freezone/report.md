# Mansha origin check: wooden bedroom furniture

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

Your wooden bedroom furniture meets the rule of origin, so the CEPA rate can be claimed. Claiming the CEPA rate reduces the Basic Customs Duty from 20% to 12.5%, saving an estimated 7500.00 AED on the 100,000 AED shipment. Follow the claim checklist below to obtain the preferential rate.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 94036000, Other wooden furniture, with high confidence. Confirm this code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3][4]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| 20% | 12.5% | 7500.00 AED |

The normal (MFN) rate is 20% and the CEPA rate is 12.5%. The estimated saving is 7500.00 AED. [1][2][3][4]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff heading (CTH) and at least 40% value addition (VA). The final manufacture check passed, meaning the last manufacturing step occurred in the UAE. The minimal operations check passed, indicating the work goes beyond the simple operations that alone would not confer origin. The CTH check passed because all non‑originating inputs are classified outside heading 9403, and the VA check passed with a build‑down of 52.0% on an FOB basis, exceeding the 40% threshold. [5]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] Register the factory with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism through the Factory Registration service, which requires the factory to be located outside a free zone, a MoIAT industrial licence, and a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added; processing takes about 3 working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [6][7]
- [ ] Prepare the documents needed for the certificate application: proof of factory registration, the commercial invoice, and, if a trading exporter, the factory's purchase invoice.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] Log in to the ministry e-services portal and choose the Certificate of Origin for India service.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] Fill in the application form with the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product; each item must qualify separately.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [11]
- [ ] Upload the required documents.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] Pay the certificate fee: AED 50 (band: invoice value up to AED 100000).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] Submit the application.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] Receive the approval, or a return with comments to correct; then print the approved certificate. Processing takes about one working day.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] Ensure the certificate is issued prior to, at, or within 5 working days of the date of exportation. It is valid for 12 months from issue and covers only one import.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [11][12]
- [ ] If needed, a certificate can be issued retrospectively up to 12 months from the date of shipment, marked 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9 (Remarks) of India's certificate form.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [11][12]
- [ ] Ship the goods directly from the UAE to India. Transit through another country is allowed only under customs control, with no operations other than unloading, reloading, storage or similar. If a third party issues the invoice, the exporter must indicate 'third-party invoicing'.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [11][12]
- [ ] Give the certificate to the Indian importer, who will claim the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry. The importer must hold origin information and keep supporting documents for at least 5 years from filing, and answer a customs request within 10 working days.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [13][14][15]
- [ ] Keep origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least 5 years starting from the end of the year of the certificate's issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [11][12]

## Important notes

- The '+' in the rule is read as 'and' (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [5]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [5]
- Factory declared in a free zone: likely not eligible for the ministry's factory registration; confirm with the ministry. [6][7]
- The duty estimate is on the shipment value given. India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. [2][3][4]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3][4]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [5][11][12]
- Interpretation: de minimis helps only the tariff-shift part of a rule, not the VA percentage. [11][12]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [7][8][10][11][12][16][17]
- Electronic certificates are reportedly accepted and uploaded to India's e-Sanchit system, but this is from secondary sources, not confirmed. [13][14][15]
- Inference: SWS is charged on the reduced BCD (no CEPA-specific text found). [15][18]
- Inference: anti-dumping and safeguard duties still apply (no exemption found). [15][18]
- The CEPA rate reduces Basic Customs Duty only; IGST, GST compensation cess, Social Welfare Surcharge and any anti‑dumping or safeguard duty are still payable. [15][18]
- The agreement refers to box 8 on the certificate, but India's origin rules and certificate form require box 9; India's form governs entry into India. [11][12]
