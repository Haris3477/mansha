# Mansha origin check: aluminium window frames

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The aluminium window frames meet the rule of origin, so the CEPA rate can be claimed. This results in an estimated saving of 10000.00 AED.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 76101000, Doors, windows and their frames and thresholds for doors (aluminium). Mansha's confidence in this classification is high, but you should confirm the code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate is 10% and the CEPA rate is 0%. This results in an estimated saving of 10000.00 AED. Note that the CEPA rate applies only to Basic Customs Duty; other import charges such as IGST are still payable. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH) and at least 45% value addition (VA) on a free-on-board (FOB) basis. The final manufacture check passed, confirming the last step took place in the UAE, and the minimal operations check passed, showing the work went beyond simple operations. The CTSH check passed because all three non-originating inputs are classified outside subheading 761010, and the VA check passed with a build-down of 49.0%, exceeding the 45% threshold. Therefore the product meets the rule of origin. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] Register the factory with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism (if not already registered). The factory must be located outside a free zone, hold a MoIAT industrial licence, and provide a letter confirming 40% domestic value added; processing takes about three working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] Log in to the ministry e-services portal and select the Certificate of Origin for India service.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Complete the application form with the HS code, description, quantity and value of the product; each item must qualify separately.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [10]
- [ ] Upload the required documents: proof of factory registration, the commercial invoice, and, if a trading exporter, the factory's purchase invoice.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Pay the certificate fee online. The fee for an invoice value up to AED 100,000 is AED 50.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Submit the application. Processing takes about one working day.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The certificate must be issued before, at, or within five working days of export; it is valid for twelve months from issue and covers one import.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [10][11]
- [ ] Print the approved certificate.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Ship the goods directly from the UAE to India; transit through another country is allowed only under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading or storage.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]
- [ ] Provide the certificate to the Indian importer, who will claim the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and must hold origin information under CAROTAR 2020.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] Keep origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least five years from the end of the calendar year of the certificate's issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]

## Important notes

- The duty estimate is based on the shipment value given (100,000 AED). India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. Shown in the shipment's own currency; no conversion is done. [2][3]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, the Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- The '+' in the product-specific rule is interpreted as 'and' (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- The origin checks (final manufacture and minimal operations) are based on the exporter's own declaration. [4]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' (used in the build-up method) are not defined in the agreement. [4][10][11]
- Interpretation: de minimis helps only the tariff-shift part of a rule, not the VA percentage. [10][11]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][9][10][11][15][16]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, which is required before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- The Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) is likely charged on the reduced BCD (no CEPA-specific text on it was found). [14][17]
- Anti-dumping and safeguard duties likely still apply (no CEPA exemption from them was found). [14][17]
- A product that only undergoes the minimal operations listed in Article 3.5.1 (e.g., simple assembly, packaging, labelling) does not originate, even if it meets its product-specific rule. [10][11]
- Electronic certificates from the UAE issuing authority are accepted and uploaded to e-Sanchit by the importer or customs broker. [12][13][14]

## What's still needed

- Is your factory located in a free zone? (Free-zone factories may not be eligible for the required factory registration.)
- Can you confirm that the final manufacture in the UAE went beyond the minimal operations listed in Article 3.5.1 of the agreement? (This is based on your declaration.)
