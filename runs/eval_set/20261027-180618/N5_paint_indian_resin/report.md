# Mansha origin check: acrylic paint

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The product meets the rule of origin, so the CEPA rate can be claimed. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. See the checklist below for the steps to claim. Caution: this result counts your inputs from India as originating, which holds only with proof of their originating status; without that proof they count as non-originating inputs. Keep that proof before relying on this result.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 32091010, described as Acrylic emulsion (paint). Mansha's confidence in this classification is high, but you should confirm the code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| tariff 10%, effective 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

The India-UAE CEPA applies. The normal (MFN) rate is 10% and the CEPA rate is 0%. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH) for each non-originating input and value addition (VA) of at least 40% on a FOB basis. The last manufacturing step took place in the UAE, and the work done goes beyond the simple operations that alone would not confer origin. Both non-originating inputs are classified outside subheading 320910, satisfying the CTSH requirement. The value addition is 65.0% on a FOB basis, exceeding the 40% threshold, so the product meets the rule of origin. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] Register the factory through the Ministry of Economy and Tourism's Factory Registration service (moet.gov.ae) before applying for a certificate; registration requires the factory to be located outside a free zone, a MoIAT industrial licence, and a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added, and takes about three working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] Apply for a preferential Certificate of Origin for India via the 'Certificate of Origin for India' e-service: log in to the ministry e-services portal, choose the service, fill in the application form, upload the required documents (proof of factory registration, commercial invoice, and for a trading exporter the factory's purchase invoice), pay the fee online, submit the application, and receive approval or a return with comments to correct; then print the approved certificate.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Pay the certificate fee of AED 50 (band: invoice value up to AED 100000) as charged by the Ministry of Foreign Trade.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The certificate is issued prior to, at, or within five working days of export, is valid for twelve months from the date of issue, and covers only one import (it may list several products). It must bear the authorised signature and official seal of the competent authority.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [10][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry, holds origin information under CAROTAR 2020 including which origin criterion is claimed, keeps supporting documents for at least five years from filing, and must answer a customs request within ten working days.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter must keep origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least five years starting from the end of the calendar year of the certificate's issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]
- [ ] The goods must be shipped directly from the UAE to India; transit through another country is allowed only under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading, storage or similar. An invoice issued by a third party does not by itself cause the claim to be denied, but the exporter must indicate 'third-party invoicing'.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]

## Important notes

- The '+' in the rule is read as 'and' (both tests must pass); the agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- Indian inputs count as originating only with proof of their originating status (bilateral cumulation, Art. 3.7). [4]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [4]
- Estimate on the shipment value given. India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. Shown in the shipment's own currency; never converted. [2][3]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, SWS and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [4][10][11]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][9][10][11][15][16]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- The Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) is likely charged on the reduced BCD (no CEPA-specific text on it was found). [14][17]
- Anti-dumping and safeguard duties likely still apply (no CEPA exemption from them was found). [14][17]
- For a retrospective certificate, the agreement specifies box 8 for the 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' mark, while India's form uses box 9. India's form governs entry into India. [10][11]
- The CEPA requires document retention for 5 years, even though UAE law sets a minimum of 3 years. [10][11]
