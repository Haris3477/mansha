# Mansha origin check: aluminium window frames

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The aluminium window frames meet the rule of origin under the India-UAE CEPA, so you can claim the preferential CEPA rate of 0% for your shipment to India. This reduces the normal duty rate from 10% to 0%, resulting in an estimated saving of 10000.00 AED for your shipment valued at 100000 AED. Please proceed with the checklist below to claim this preference.

## Product classification

The product is classified under Indian tariff line 76101000, which covers doors, windows and their frames and thresholds for doors (aluminium). Mansha's confidence in this classification is high, but you should still confirm the code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| tariff 10%, effective 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate is 10% and the CEPA rate is 0% for this product. By claiming the CEPA rate, you can save an estimated 10000.00 AED on your shipment. Please note that the CEPA rate applies only to Basic Customs Duty, so other import charges such as IGST are still payable even when the CEPA rate is 0%. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

To qualify for the CEPA rate, the product must meet the rule of origin, which requires a Change in Tariff Subheading (CTSH) and at least 45% Value Addition (VA). CTSH means that all non-originating inputs must be classified under a different 6-digit subheading than the final product, which was satisfied here. The Value Addition (VA) represents the share of the product's price that does not come from non-originating materials, calculated on an FOB basis. All checks passed: the final manufacture took place in the UAE, the work done exceeded minimal operations, the CTSH requirement was met, and the VA build-down reached 49.0%, which is above the 45% threshold. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] Register the factory with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. The factory must be outside a free zone, hold a MoIAT industrial licence and a letter confirming 40% domestic value added; registration takes about 3 working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] Log in to the ministry e-services portal and choose the Certificate of Origin for India service.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Fill in the application form, upload proof of factory registration, the commercial invoice and (for a trading exporter) the factory's purchase invoice.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Pay the certificate fee online: AED 50 for an invoice value up to AED 100000 (fee range AED 10–200).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Submit the application; processing takes about one working day and the service runs 24/7. Approval is received, or a return with comments to correct.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Print the approved certificate. It must be issued before, at, or within 5 working days of export; a retrospective issue is possible up to 12 months from shipment with 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [10][11]
- [ ] The certificate is valid for 12 months from issue and covers one import.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [10][11]
- [ ] Provide the certificate to the Indian importer, who claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and keeps supporting documents for at least 5 years.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter must keep origin records (including cost breakdown) for at least 5 years from the end of the calendar year of issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]
- [ ] Goods must be shipped directly from the UAE to India; transit through another country is allowed only under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading, storage or similar. Third-party invoicing is allowed if the exporter indicates it.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]

## Important notes

- India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value, so actual duty amounts will differ from the estimate based on the shipment value given. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate reduces Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, Social Welfare Surcharge and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- The '+' in the rule 'CTSH + VA 45%' is read as 'and' (both tests must pass); the agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [4]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][9][10][11][15][16]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, which is required before applying for the certificate. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- Electronic certificates are reportedly accepted and uploaded to India's e-Sanchit system, but this is seen only in secondary sources (CBIC Instruction 28/2022 via LiveLaw, taxmann), not confirmed in the official text. [12][13][14]
- Interpretation: de minimis helps only the tariff-shift part of a rule, not the VA percentage. [10][11]
- When filling out the certificate of origin, use box 9 for remarks as required by India's certificate form, because India's form governs entry into India, even though the agreement text mentions box 8. [10][11]
- The 40% value-added letter is a registration condition only and is not the product-specific rule test for the CEPA, which is set per tariff line. [5][6]
