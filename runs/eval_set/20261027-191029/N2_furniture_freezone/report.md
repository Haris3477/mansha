# Mansha origin check: hardwood bedroom furniture

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The hardwood bedroom furniture qualifies for the CEPA rate under the India-UAE CEPA. This means the reduced duty rate of 12.5% can be claimed, providing an estimated saving of 7500.00 AED. Caution: your factory is in a free zone, so it is likely not eligible for the factory registration needed before applying for the certificate of origin; confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism before relying on this result. See the checklist below for the steps to claim the CEPA rate.

## Product classification

The tariff line is 94036000 for other wooden furniture, and Mansha's confidence in this classification is high. However, you should confirm the code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3][4]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| tariff 20%, effective 20% | 12.5% | 7500.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate is 20% and the CEPA rate is 12.5%. Claiming the CEPA rate provides an estimated saving of 7500.00 AED. [1][2][3][4]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff heading (CTH) and value addition (VA) of at least 40%. CTH means non-originating inputs must be classified in a different 4-digit heading than the product, and VA is the share of the product's price that does not come from non-originating materials. The product meets the rule because the final manufacture and minimal operations checks passed, all non-originating inputs are classified outside heading 9403, and the value addition is 52.0% on a FOB basis, which exceeds the 40% threshold. [5]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] The Ministry of Economy and Tourism registers the factory (requires the factory to be located outside a free zone, a MoIAT industrial licence, and a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added; takes about 3 working days).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [6][7]  
  **Your factory is in a free zone: see the caution in the summary.**
- [ ] The exporter prepares the application documents: proof of factory registration, the commercial invoice, and for a trading exporter, the factory's purchase invoice.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] The exporter logs in to the ministry e-services portal, chooses the Certificate of Origin for India service, fills in the application form, uploads the required documents, and pays the fee online.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade charges a certificate fee of AED 50 (band: invoice value up to AED 100000).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] The exporter submits the application and receives approval, or a return with comments to correct, then prints the approved certificate.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] The competent authority issues the certificate prior to, at, or within five working days of the date of exportation.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [11][12]
- [ ] The certificate is valid for twelve months from the date of issue and covers only one import.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [11][12]
- [ ] The exporter ensures the goods are shipped directly from the UAE to India; if in transit, only under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading, storage or similar.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [11][12]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry, holds origin information under CAROTAR 2020, and keeps supporting documents for at least five years from filing.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [13][14][15]
- [ ] The exporter keeps origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least five years starting from the end of the calendar year of the date of issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [11][12]

## Important notes

- The factory is declared in a free zone: likely not eligible for the ministry's factory registration; confirm with the ministry. [5]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [5]
- '+' in the rule is read as 'and' (both tests must pass); the agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [5]
- The duty estimate is on the shipment value given; India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. [2][3][4]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only; IGST, GST compensation cess, the Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3][4]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3][4]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [5][11][12]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [7][8][10][11][12][16][17]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India; confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [6][7]
- The Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) is likely charged on the reduced BCD (no CEPA-specific text on it was found). [15][18]
- Anti-dumping and safeguard duties likely still apply (no CEPA exemption from them was found). [15][18]
- UAE law sets a shorter 3-year minimum for record keeping, but the CEPA's 5-year requirement governs CEPA claims. [11][12]
- The issuing authority may apply risk management, including asking for detailed cost sheets and visiting the factory. [11]
