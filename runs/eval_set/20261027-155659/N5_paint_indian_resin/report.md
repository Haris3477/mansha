# Mansha origin check: acrylic paint

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The product meets the rule of origin, so the CEPA rate can be claimed. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. Please refer to the checklist below for the steps to claim. Caution: this result counts your inputs from India as originating, which holds only with proof of their originating status; without that proof they count as non-originating inputs. Keep that proof before relying on this result.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 32091010, Acrylic emulsion (paint). Mansha’s confidence in this classification is high, but you should confirm it with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate is 10% and the CEPA rate is 0%. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH) and at least 40% value addition (VA). The final manufacture and minimal operations checks passed, meaning the last manufacturing step took place in the UAE and the work goes beyond simple operations. The CTSH requirement is satisfied because all non-originating inputs are classified outside subheading 320910, and the VA requirement is met with a value addition of 65.0% on an FOB basis, exceeding the 40% threshold. As a result, the product meets the rule of origin. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] Register the factory with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism through the Factory Registration service. The factory must be located outside a free zone, have a MoIAT industrial licence, and a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added. Registration takes about 3 working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] Log in to the ministry e-services portal and choose the Certificate of Origin for India service.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Fill in the application form, including the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product. Each item must qualify separately. The issuing authority may ask for detailed cost sheets and conduct a factory visit.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [10]
- [ ] Upload the required documents: proof of factory registration, the commercial invoice, and for a trading exporter, the factory's purchase invoice.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Pay the certificate fee: AED 50 (band: invoice value up to AED 100000).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Submit the application.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Receive approval, or a return with comments to correct. If approved, print the approved certificate.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The certificate must be issued prior to, at, or within five working days of the date of exportation. It is valid for twelve months from the date of issue and covers only one import.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [10][11]
- [ ] If the invoice is issued by a third party, the exporter must indicate 'third-party invoicing'.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and must hold origin information, including which origin criterion is claimed. The importer keeps supporting documents for at least 5 years from filing and must answer a customs request within 10 working days.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] Keep origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least five years starting from the end of the calendar year of the certificate's issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]

## Important notes

- Mansha's interpretation: '+' in the rule is read as 'and' (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define '+'. [4]
- Indian inputs count as originating only with proof of their originating status (bilateral cumulation, Art. 3.7). [4]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [4]
- Estimate on the shipment value given. India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. Shown in the shipment's own currency; never converted. [2][3]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, SWS and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [4][10][11]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][9][10][11][15][16]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- Electronic certificates are reportedly accepted and uploaded to India's e-Sanchit system, but this is seen only in secondary sources (CBIC Instruction 28/2022 via LiveLaw, taxmann), not the official text. [12][13][14]
- There is a discrepancy between the agreement and India's certificate form: the agreement says box 8, but India's origin rules and certificate form say box 9; India's form governs entry into India. [10][11]
- UAE law sets a 3-year minimum for record keeping, but the CEPA requires 5 years for CEPA claims. [10][11]
