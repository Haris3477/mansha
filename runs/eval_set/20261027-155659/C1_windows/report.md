# Mansha origin check: aluminium window frames

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The aluminium window frames meet the rule of origin, so the CEPA rate of 0% can be claimed. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. See the claim checklist below for the required steps.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 76101000, Doors, windows and their frames and thresholds for doors (aluminium). Mansha's confidence in this classification is high, but you should confirm the code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

The India-UAE CEPA sets a normal (MFN) rate of 10% and a CEPA rate of 0% for this product. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH) and at least 45% value addition (VA) on an FOB basis. The final manufacture in the UAE passes, and the manufacturing goes beyond minimal operations. All three non‑originating inputs are classified outside subheading 761010, satisfying CTSH. The value added is 49.0%, which exceeds the 45% threshold, satisfying VA. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] The Ministry of Economy and Tourism requires factory registration before a certificate of origin can be applied for. The factory must be located outside a free zone, hold a MoIAT industrial licence, and have a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added. Registration takes about 3 working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade offers the Certificate of Origin for India e-service. Log in to the ministry e-services portal, choose the Certificate of Origin for India service, fill in the application form, upload proof of factory registration and the commercial invoice (and for a trading exporter, the factory's purchase invoice), pay the fee online, and submit. Processing takes about one working day.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade charges a certificate fee of AED 50 for an invoice value of AED 100000 (fee range: AED 10 to AED 200).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The competent authority issues the certificate. It must be issued before, at, or within 5 working days of export, is valid for 12 months from issue, and covers one import. A certificate may be issued retrospectively up to 12 months from shipment, marked 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9 of India's certificate form.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [10][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and must hold origin information under CAROTAR 2020, including which origin criterion is claimed. The importer keeps supporting documents for at least 5 years from filing and must answer a customs request within 10 working days.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter keeps origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least 5 years starting from the end of the calendar year of the certificate's issue. India may ask the UAE authority to verify a certificate; the authority answers an origin verification request within 90 days, extendable by 60. Refusing a verification visit is grounds for denying preferential benefits.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]

## Important notes

- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][9][10][11][15][16]
- India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual duty amounts will differ from the estimate shown. The estimate is in the shipment's own currency and has not been converted. [2][3]
- Operations and final manufacture in the UAE are the exporter's own declaration. [4]
- The '+' in the rule is read as 'and' (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. [5][6]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [4][10][11]
- Electronic certificates are reportedly accepted and uploaded to India's e-Sanchit system, but this is seen only in secondary sources (CBIC Instruction 28/2022, read through LiveLaw and taxmann), not the official text. [12][13][14]
- The 0% CEPA rate applies only to Basic Customs Duty and the Agriculture Infrastructure and Development Cess. Other charges such as IGST, GST compensation cess, Social Welfare Surcharge, and any anti‑dumping, countervailing or safeguard duties remain payable. [14][17]
- You must keep records for at least five years to support a CEPA claim, even though UAE law requires only three years. [10][11]
