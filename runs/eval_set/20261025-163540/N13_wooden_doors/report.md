## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] The factory must be registered through the Ministry of Economy and Tourism's Factory Registration service before applying for a certificate of origin; registration requires a factory located outside a free zone, a MoIAT industrial licence, and a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added, and takes about 3 working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [6][7]
- [ ] The exporter applies for the certificate of origin through the Ministry of Foreign Trade's 'Certificate of Origin for India' e-service by logging in, filling the form, uploading required documents (proof of factory registration, commercial invoice, and for a trading exporter, the factory's purchase invoice), paying the fee online, and submitting; processing takes about one working day.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] The application must include the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product, and each item must qualify separately; the issuing authority may ask for detailed cost sheets and conduct a factory visit.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [11]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade charges a certificate fee of AED 10 to AED 200, depending on the invoice value in AED.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] The certificate of origin is valid for 12 months from issue, covers only one import, and must be issued before, at, or within 5 working days of export; it can be issued retrospectively up to 12 months from shipment with 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [11][12]
- [ ] The exporter must ship directly from the UAE to India; if transit through another country is necessary, the goods must remain under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading, or storage. Third-party invoicing is allowed but must be indicated.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [11][12]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry, holds origin information including which origin criterion is claimed, and keeps supporting documents for at least 5 years from filing; the importer must answer customs requests within 10 working days.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [13][14][15]
- [ ] The exporter must keep origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least 5 years starting from the end of the calendar year of the certificate's issue; refusal to consent to a verification visit is grounds for denying preferential benefits.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [11][12]

## Important notes

- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, SWS and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3][4]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3][4]
- '+' in a rule is read as 'and' (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [5]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [5][11][12]
- Interpretation: de minimis helps only the tariff-shift part of a rule, not the VA percentage. [11][12]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [7][8][10][11][12][16][17]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [6][7]
- No usable shipment value (positive amount and currency), so only rates are given. [2][3][4]
- The CEPA reduces the duty but does not eliminate it. [1][2][3][4]
- The agreement mentions box 8, but India's origin rules and certificate form require box 9, and the Indian form governs entry into India. [11][12]
