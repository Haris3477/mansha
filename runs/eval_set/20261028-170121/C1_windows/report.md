# Mansha origin check: aluminium window frames

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The aluminium window frames qualify for the preferential duty rate under the India-UAE CEPA because they meet the rule of origin. By claiming the CEPA rate on this 100000 AED shipment, you can expect an estimated saving of 10000.00 AED in customs duty. See the checklist below for the steps to claim the CEPA rate.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 76101000, which covers doors, windows and their frames and thresholds for doors (aluminium). Mansha's confidence in this classification is high, but you should confirm the code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 0% (2026 CEPA rate) | 10000.00 AED |

The normal (MFN) rate for this product is 10%, while the CEPA rate is 0% (2026 CEPA rate). The CEPA rate is the reduced rate under the trade agreement, which applies to Basic Customs Duty only, so other import charges such as IGST are still paid even when the CEPA rate is 0%. The estimated saving on this shipment is 10000.00 AED. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH), meaning each non-originating input must be classified in a different 6-digit subheading than the product, and value addition (VA) of at least 45%, which is the share of the product's price that does not come from non-originating materials calculated on an FOB basis (the price of the goods loaded on board at the UAE port). The product meets this rule because the final manufacture check passed, confirming the last step took place in the UAE, and the minimal operations check passed, meaning the work goes beyond the simple operations that never give origin on their own. Specifically, the CTSH check passed as all 3 non-originating inputs are classified outside subheading 761010, and the VA check passed with a build-down of 49.0% against the 45% threshold on an FOB basis. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] The Ministry of Economy and Tourism registers the factory. The factory must be located outside a free zone, hold a MoIAT industrial licence, and have a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added. Registration takes about 3 working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] The issuing authority requires the certificate application to include the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product; each item must qualify separately in its own right. The authority may apply risk management, including asking for detailed cost sheets and conducting a factory visit.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [7]
- [ ] The exporter applies through the Certificate of Origin for India e-service: logs in to the ministry e-services portal, chooses the Certificate of Origin for India service, fills in the application form, uploads proof of factory registration, the commercial invoice (and for a trading exporter, the factory's purchase invoice), pays the fee online, submits the application, and prints the approved certificate. Processing takes about one working day; the service runs 24/7.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [6][8][9][10]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade charges a certificate fee of AED 50 for an invoice value up to AED 100000.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][8][9][10]
- [ ] The competent authority issues the certificate prior to, at, or within five working days of the date of exportation. The certificate is valid for twelve months from the date of issue and covers only one import (it may list several products). It may be issued retrospectively up to twelve months from the date of shipment, marked 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [7][11]
- [ ] The exporter ships the goods directly from the UAE to India. Transit through another country is allowed only under customs control, with no operations other than unloading, reloading, storage or similar. An invoice issued by a third party does not by itself cause the claim to be denied, but the exporter must indicate 'third-party invoicing'.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and must hold origin information under CAROTAR 2020, including which origin criterion is claimed. The importer keeps supporting documents for at least five years from the date of filing and must answer a customs request within ten working days. Electronic certificates from the UAE issuing authority are accepted and uploaded to e-Sanchit by the importer or customs broker.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter must keep origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least five years, starting from the end of the year of the date of the certificate's issue. Refusing a verification visit is grounds for denying preferential benefits. India may ask the UAE authority to verify a certificate; the authority answers an origin verification request within ninety days, extendable by sixty.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][11]

## Important notes

- The duty estimate is on the Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, the Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ from the estimate. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- The origin check relies on the exporter's own declaration that final manufacture took place in the UAE and that the manufacturing goes beyond the minimal operations. [4]
- The '+' in the rule 'CTSH + VA 45%' is read as 'and' (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- The value-addition percentage is on an FOB basis; on an Ex-Works basis the threshold is 5 percentage points lower. 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [4][7][11]
- By interpretation, de minimis helps only the tariff-shift part of a rule, not the VA percentage. [7][11]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, which is required before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][8][10][11][15][16]
- The Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) is likely charged on the reduced BCD (no CEPA-specific text on it was found). Anti-dumping and safeguard duties likely still apply. [14][17]
- UAE law sets a shorter 3-year minimum for record keeping, but the CEPA's 5-year requirement governs CEPA claims. [7][11]
- Preference is given only to originating goods shipped directly from the UAE to India. Transit through another country is allowed only under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading, storage or similar. [7][11]
