# Mansha origin check: acrylic paint

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The product meets the rule of origin, so the CEPA rate can be claimed. Claiming the CEPA rate for this acrylic paint shipment to India qualifies it for a 0% duty rate, saving an estimated 10000.00 AED compared to the normal rate. Caution: this result counts your inputs from India as originating, which holds only with proof of their originating status; without that proof they count as non-originating inputs. Keep that proof before relying on this result. See the checklist below for the steps to claim the CEPA rate.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 32091010 for Acrylic emulsion (paint), and Mansha's confidence in this classification is high. However, you should confirm this tariff code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| tariff 10%, effective 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate for tariff line 32091010 is 10%, while the CEPA rate is 0%. Claiming the CEPA rate results in an estimated saving of 10000.00 AED on this shipment. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH), meaning non-originating inputs must be classified under a different subheading than the product, plus 40% value addition (VA), which is the share of the price not from non-originating materials. The product meets this rule because the final manufacture took place in the UAE and the work goes beyond minimal operations. Additionally, the change in tariff subheading (CTSH) check passed as all 2 non-originating inputs are classified outside subheading 320910. The value addition (VA) check also passed, reaching 65.0% on an FOB basis, which exceeds the 40% threshold. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] The Ministry of Economy and Tourism registers the factory (the exporter must apply; registration takes about 3 working days and requires the factory to be outside a free zone, a MoIAT industrial licence, and a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] The exporter ensures the final manufacture of the goods takes place in the UAE.  
  *Authority: Not in Mansha's sources.* [7][8]
- [ ] The exporter ships the goods directly from the UAE to India; if transit through another country is necessary, the goods must remain under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading, storage or similar. If a third party issues the invoice, the exporter must indicate 'third-party invoicing'.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8]
- [ ] The exporter logs in to the ministry e-services portal and chooses the Certificate of Origin for India service.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [6][9][10][11]
- [ ] The issuing authority requires the application to include the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product; each item must qualify separately in its own right.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [7]
- [ ] The exporter uploads proof of factory registration, the commercial invoice, and, for a trading exporter, the factory's purchase invoice.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [6][9][10][11]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade charges a certificate fee of AED 50 (band: invoice value up to AED 100000; the service fee range is AED 10 to AED 200).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][9][10][11]
- [ ] The exporter submits the application.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [6][9][10][11]
- [ ] The competent authority issues the certificate before, at, or within five working days of the date of exportation; it is valid for twelve months from issue and covers only one import. If the certificate is issued after export, it may be marked 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9 (Remarks) of India's certificate form, up to twelve months from the date of shipment.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [7][8]
- [ ] The exporter prints the approved certificate.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [6][9][10][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and keeps supporting documents for at least five years from filing; the importer must answer a customs request within ten working days.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter keeps origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least five years starting from the end of the calendar year of the certificate's issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8]

## Important notes

- Estimate on the shipment value given. India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. Shown in the shipment's own currency; never converted. [2][3]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, the Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- '+' in a rule is read as 'and' (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- Indian inputs count as originating only with proof of their originating status (bilateral cumulation, Art. 3.7). [4]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [4]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [4][7][8]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][8][9][11][15][16]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- The Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) is likely charged on the reduced BCD (no CEPA-specific text on it was found). [14][17]
- Anti-dumping and safeguard duties likely still apply (no CEPA exemption from them was found). [14][17]
- By interpretation, de minimis helps only the tariff-shift part of a rule, not the VA percentage. [7][8]
- UAE law sets a shorter 3-year minimum, while the CEPA's 5 years governs CEPA claims. [7][8]
- Minor typing or formatting errors on the certificate are tolerated, but discrepancies in signature or seal are not. [7][8]
