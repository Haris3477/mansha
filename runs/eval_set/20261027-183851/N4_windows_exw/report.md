# Mansha origin check: aluminium windows

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The aluminium windows meet the rule of origin, so the CEPA rate can be claimed. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. Refer to the claim checklist below for the required steps.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 76101000, described as doors, windows and their frames and thresholds for doors (aluminium). Mansha’s confidence in this classification is medium, so you should confirm the code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India‑UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate is 10% and the CEPA rate is 0%, which applies only to Basic Customs Duty. Other import charges such as IGST are still payable. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH) and a value addition (VA) of at least 45%. CTSH means each non‑originating input must be classified in a different 6‑digit subheading, while VA is the share of the product’s price that does not come from non‑originating materials. The final manufacturing step was performed in the UAE and the work goes beyond the minimal operations listed in the agreement, so those checks are satisfied. All three non‑originating inputs are classified outside subheading 761010, fulfilling CTSH, and the value addition on an Ex‑Works basis is 43.0%, which exceeds the required 40% after the 5‑point reduction for Ex‑Works. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] Register the factory with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism through the Factory Registration service. The factory must be located outside a free zone, have a MoIAT industrial licence, and a letter confirming 40% domestic value added. Processing takes about 3 working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] Prepare the required documents: proof of factory registration, the commercial invoice, and for a trading exporter, the factory's purchase invoice.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Submit the certificate application electronically via the 'Certificate of Origin for India' e-service (or the Ministry of Foreign Trade's 'Issue a Certificate Of Origin' service), filling in the form, uploading the documents, and paying the fee online.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The application must include the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product. Each item must qualify separately. The issuing authority may request detailed cost sheets and conduct a factory visit.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [10]
- [ ] Pay the certificate fee: AED 50 (for invoice value up to AED 100000).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Receive the approved certificate. It should be issued prior to, at, or within 5 working days of export, and is valid for 12 months from issue for one import. A certificate can be issued retrospectively up to 12 months from shipment, marked 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9 of India's certificate form.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [10][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and uploads the certificate to e-Sanchit. The importer must hold origin information under CAROTAR 2020, keep supporting documents for at least 5 years from filing, and answer customs requests within 10 working days.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter must keep origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least 5 years from the end of the calendar year of issue, and must consent to verification visits; refusal is grounds for denying preferential benefits.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]

## Important notes

- The duty estimate is based on the shipment value given. India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. Shown in the shipment's own currency; never converted. [2][3]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, the Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- The '+' in the rule 'CTSH + VA 45%' is read as 'and' (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [4]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. The agreement does not say whether the build-down and build-up methods use the same threshold, apart from the 5-point Ex-Works adjustment. [4][10][11]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][9][10][11][15][16]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- The Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) is likely charged on the reduced BCD (no CEPA-specific text on it was found). Anti-dumping and safeguard duties likely still apply (no CEPA exemption from them was found). [14][17]
- The HS code provided (76101090) does not match any tariff line exactly; the closest match is 76101000. You should confirm the correct code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]
- Preferential treatment applies only if the goods are shipped directly from the UAE to India. Transit through another country is allowed only under customs control and without any operations other than unloading, reloading or storage. If a third party issues the invoice, you must indicate 'third‑party invoicing' on the certificate. [10][11]
