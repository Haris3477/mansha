# Mansha origin check: aluminium window frames

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The product meets the rule of origin, so the CEPA rate can be claimed. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 76101000, which covers doors, windows and their frames and thresholds for doors (aluminium). Mansha's confidence in this classification is high, but you should confirm the code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| tariff 10%, effective 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India‑UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate is 10% and the CEPA rate is 0%. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin is CTSH plus VA 45%. CTSH (change in tariff subheading) requires each non‑originating input to be classified in a different six‑digit subheading than the product, while VA (value addition) requires that at least 45% of the product's price, calculated on an FOB basis, does not come from non‑originating materials. The product meets this rule because the final manufacture took place in the UAE, the operations performed go beyond minimal activities, all three non‑originating inputs are classified outside subheading 761010, and the build‑down value addition is 49.0%, which exceeds the 45% threshold. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] The Ministry of Economy and Tourism registers the factory (located outside a free zone, with a MoIAT industrial licence and a letter confirming 40% domestic value added) before the exporter can apply for a certificate of origin for India.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] The exporter logs in to the ministry e-services portal, chooses the Certificate of Origin for India service, fills in the application form, uploads the required documents (proof of factory registration, commercial invoice, and for a trading exporter the factory's purchase invoice), and submits the application.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade charges a certificate fee of AED 50 for invoice values up to AED 100000.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The issuing authority may require the application to include the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product, and each item must qualify separately; it may also ask for detailed cost sheets and conduct a factory visit.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [10]
- [ ] The competent authority issues the certificate before, at, or within five working days of export; the certificate is valid for twelve months from issue and covers only one import. If issued retrospectively, it must be marked 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9 of India's certificate form.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [10][11]
- [ ] The exporter ensures the goods are shipped directly from the UAE to India; if transit through another country is necessary, the goods must remain under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading, storage or similar.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry, holds origin information including which origin criterion is claimed, and keeps supporting documents for at least five years from filing.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter keeps origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least five years from the end of the calendar year of the certificate issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]

## Important notes

- Estimate on the shipment value given. India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. Shown in the shipment's own currency; never converted. [2][3]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, the Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- '+' in a rule is read as 'and' (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [4]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [4][10][11]
- The Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) is likely charged on the reduced BCD (no CEPA-specific text on it was found). [14][15]
- Anti-dumping and safeguard duties likely still apply (no CEPA exemption from them was found). [14][15]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][9][10][11][16][17]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- The agreement does not clarify whether the build‑down and build‑up methods use the same value‑addition threshold, apart from a 5‑point Ex‑Works adjustment. [4][10][11]
- For a retrospective certificate, the agreement specifies box 8 for the retrospective mark, but India's origin rules and certificate form require box 9; India's form governs entry into India. [10][11]
