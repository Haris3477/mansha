# Mansha origin check: wooden bedroom furniture

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The wooden bedroom furniture meets the rule of origin, so the CEPA rate can be claimed under the India-UAE CEPA. The CEPA rate is the reduced rate under the trade agreement, which applies to Basic Customs Duty only. On a shipment value of 100000 AED, this qualifies for an estimated saving of 7500.00 AED. Caution: your factory is in a free zone, so it is likely not eligible for the factory registration needed before applying for the certificate of origin; confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism before relying on this result.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 94036000 for Other wooden furniture. Mansha's confidence in this classification is high, but you should confirm the code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3][4]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| tariff 20%, effective 20% | 12.5% | 7500.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate is 20% and the CEPA rate is 12.5%. The estimated saving is 7500.00 AED. [1][2][3][4]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff heading (CTH), where each non-originating input must be in a different 4-digit heading than the product, and value addition (VA) of at least 40% on an FOB basis. The product meets this rule because the final manufacture took place in the UAE and the work goes beyond minimal operations. The CTH check passed as all 2 non-originating inputs are outside heading 9403, and the VA check passed with a build-down of 52.0% against the 40% threshold. [5]

- [ ] The Ministry of Economy and Tourism requires that the factory be registered through its Factory Registration service (moet.gov.ae) before applying for a certificate of origin.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [6][7]

- [ ] The exporter logs in to the ministry e-services portal.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] The exporter chooses the Certificate of Origin for India service.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] The issuing authority requires that the application include the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product, and that each item qualify separately.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [11]
- [ ] The exporter fills in the application form.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] The exporter uploads the required documents: proof of factory registration, the commercial invoice, and for a trading exporter, the factory's purchase invoice.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] The exporter pays the certificate fee online.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] The exporter submits the application.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] The exporter receives approval, or a return with comments to correct.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] The exporter prints the approved certificate.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade charges a certificate fee of AED 50 (band: invoice value up to AED 100000).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] The competent authority issues the certificate prior to, at or within five working days of the date of exportation.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [11][12]
- [ ] The competent authority states that the certificate is valid for twelve (12) months from the date of issue and covers only one import.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [11][12]
- [ ] The competent authority allows retrospective issuance up to 12 months from the date of shipment, marking 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9 of India's certificate form.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [11][12]
- [ ] The exporter ensures the goods are shipped directly from the UAE to India; if in transit, under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading, storage or similar.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [11][12]
- [ ] The exporter indicates 'third-party invoicing' if the invoice is issued by a third party.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [11][12]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and holds origin information under CAROTAR 2020.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [13][14][15]
- [ ] The exporter keeps origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least five (5) years from the end of the calendar year of the certificate's issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [11][12]

## Important notes

- Mansha interprets '+' in the rule as 'and' (both tests must pass); the agreement does not define '+'. [5]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [5]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, which is required before applying for the certificate; confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [6][7]
- India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual duty amounts will differ from the estimate. [2][3][4]
- The estimate is shown in the shipment's own currency and is not converted. [2][3][4]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, the Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3][4]
- The Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) is likely charged on the reduced BCD (no CEPA-specific text on it was found). [15][16]
- Anti-dumping and safeguard duties likely still apply (no CEPA exemption from them was found). [15][16]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [5][11][12]
- By interpretation, de minimis helps only the tariff-shift part of a rule, not the VA percentage. [11][12]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [7][8][10][11][12][17][18]
- For retrospective certificate issuance, use box 9 for the 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' mark, as India's form governs entry into India. [11][12]
- The issuing authority may apply risk management, which can include asking for detailed cost sheets and conducting a factory visit. [11]
