## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] Register the factory with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism (must be outside a free zone, with a MoIAT industrial licence and a letter confirming 40% domestic value added).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] Log in to the ministry e-services portal and choose the Certificate of Origin for India service.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Fill in the application form with HS code, description, quantity and value; upload proof of factory registration, the commercial invoice, and for a trading exporter the factory's purchase invoice.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Pay the certificate fee online; the fee ranges from AED 10 to AED 200 depending on invoice value.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Submit the application; processing takes about one working day.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Receive the approved certificate, or a return with comments to correct.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The certificate is valid for 12 months from issue, covers one import, and must be issued before, at, or within 5 working days of export.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [10][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and keeps supporting documents for at least 5 years from filing.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] Keep origin records, including cost breakdown, for at least 5 years from the end of the calendar year of issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]

## Important notes

- The '+' in the rule is interpreted as 'and' (both tests must pass); the agreement does not define '+'. [4]
- The CEPA rate reduces Basic Customs Duty only; IGST, GST compensation cess, Social Welfare Surcharge and any anti-dumping or safeguard duties still apply. [2][3]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][9][10][11][15][16]
- Electronic certificates are reportedly accepted and uploaded to India's e-Sanchit system, but this is unconfirmed and based only on secondary sources. [12][13][14]
- Direct labour cost and direct overhead cost are not defined in the agreement. [4][10][11]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, which is required before applying for the certificate. [5][6]
- The 40% value-added requirement is for registration purposes only and does not apply to the CEPA product-specific rule test. [5][6]
