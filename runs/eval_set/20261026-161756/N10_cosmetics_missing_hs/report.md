| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| tariff 20%, effective 20% | 0% | 12000.00 AED (only if the product qualifies) |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate—the standard duty charged when no trade-agreement preference is claimed—is 20%, while the CEPA rate—the reduced rate under the trade agreement—is 0%. If the product qualifies, the estimated saving is 12000.00 AED. Note that the CEPA rate applies to Basic Customs Duty only, so other import charges such as IGST are still paid even when the CEPA rate is 0%. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH), meaning each non-originating input must be classified in a different 6-digit subheading than the product, and value addition (VA) of at least 40%, which is the share of the product's price that does not come from non-originating materials. The origin check cannot be completed yet because the bill of materials is incomplete. Specifically, the emollients in row 2 currently lack a 6-digit HS code, which is needed to check the CTSH requirement. No checks were run, so the origin result remains unconfirmed. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] Register the factory through the Ministry of Economy and Tourism's Factory Registration service. The factory must be located outside a free zone, and a MoIAT industrial licence and a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added are required. Registration takes about 3 working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] Prepare the certificate application with the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product. Each item must qualify separately. The issuing authority may apply risk management, including asking for detailed cost sheets and conducting a factory visit.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [7]
- [ ] Log in to the ministry e-services portal and choose the Certificate of Origin for India service.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][8][9][10]
- [ ] Fill in the application form and upload the required documents: proof of factory registration, the commercial invoice, and, for a trading exporter, the factory's purchase invoice.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][8][9][10]
- [ ] Pay the certificate fee online: AED 50 (fee band for invoice value up to AED 100000, based on the invoice value of 60000.00 AED).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][8][9][10]
- [ ] Submit the application. Processing takes about one working day. The application may be returned with comments to correct. Print the approved certificate.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][8][9][10]
- [ ] The certificate must be issued before, at, or within 5 working days of export. It is valid for 12 months from issue and covers only one import. A certificate may be issued retrospectively up to 12 months from shipment, marked 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9 of India's certificate form.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [7][11]
- [ ] Ship the goods directly from the UAE to India. Transit through another country is allowed only under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading or storage. If a third party issues the invoice, indicate 'third-party invoicing'.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and must hold origin information, including which origin criterion is claimed. Keep supporting documents for at least 5 years from filing and answer a customs request within 10 working days.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] Keep origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least 5 years from the end of the calendar year of the certificate's issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][11]

## Important notes

- The duty saving is estimated on the shipment value given. India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. Shown in the shipment's own currency; never converted. [2][3]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, SWS and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- The '+' in the rule 'CTSH + VA 40%' is read as 'and' (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [4][7][11]
- Interpretation: de minimis helps only the tariff-shift part of a rule, not the VA percentage. [7][11]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][8][10][11][15][16]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. [5][6]
- The operations listed in Art. 3.5.1 (a) to (q), alone or combined, never make a product originating, regardless of what the product-specific rule says. [7][11]
- The acceptance of electronic certificates and their upload to India's e-Sanchit system comes from CBIC Instruction 28/2022, which Mansha has read only through secondary reports. [12][13][14]
