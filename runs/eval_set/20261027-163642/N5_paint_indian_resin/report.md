# Mansha origin check: acrylic paint

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The product meets the rule of origin, so the CEPA rate can be claimed. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. See the checklist below for the required steps. Caution: this result counts your inputs from India as originating, which holds only with proof of their originating status; without that proof they count as non-originating inputs. Keep that proof before relying on this result.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 32091010, Acrylic emulsion (paint). Mansha's confidence in this classification is high, but you should confirm the code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate is 10% and the CEPA rate is 0%. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. Note that the CEPA rate applies only to Basic Customs Duty; other import charges such as IGST are still payable. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH) and at least 40% value addition (VA). CTSH means each non‑originating input must be classified in a different 6‑digit subheading than the product, while VA is the share of the product's price that does not come from non‑originating materials, calculated on an FOB basis. The product satisfies all checks: the final manufacture was in the UAE, the operations went beyond the minimal ones, the tariff shift condition was met, and the value addition of 65% meets the 40% threshold. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] The Ministry of Economy and Tourism requires the factory to be registered before applying for a certificate of origin; registration takes about three working days and requires the factory to be located outside a free zone, a MoIAT industrial licence, and a letter confirming 40% domestic value added.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] The application for a certificate of origin must include the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product; each item must qualify separately.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [7]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade provides the 'Certificate of Origin for India' e-service; the exporter logs in, fills in the form, uploads proof of factory registration and the commercial invoice, and submits.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][8][9][10]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade charges a certificate fee of AED 50 for an invoice value up to AED 100000 (fee range AED 10 to AED 200), paid online.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][8][9][10]
- [ ] The competent authority issues the certificate prior to, at, or within five working days of export; it is valid for twelve months from issue and covers one import.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [7][11]
- [ ] The goods must be shipped directly from the UAE to India; transit through another country is allowed only under customs control with no operations other than unloading, reloading, storage.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][11]
- [ ] The exporter sends the certificate; if a third party issues the invoice, the exporter must indicate 'third-party invoicing'.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and must hold origin information, including which origin criterion is claimed.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter keeps origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least five years from the end of the calendar year of issue, and must consent to verification visits.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][11]

## Important notes

- Estimate on the shipment value given. India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. Shown in the shipment's own currency; never converted. [2][3]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, SWS and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- '+' in a rule is read as 'and' (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- Indian inputs count as originating only with proof of their originating status (bilateral cumulation, Art. 3.7). [4]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [4]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [4][7][11]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][8][10][11][15][16]
- Inference: a producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- Inference: SWS is charged on the reduced BCD (no CEPA-specific text found). [14][17]
- Inference: anti-dumping and safeguard duties still apply (no exemption found). [14][17]
- The agreement refers to box 8, but India's origin rules and certificate form use box 9; you must follow India's form when entering the country. [7][11]
- While UAE law requires records to be kept for at least 3 years, the CEPA requires retention for 5 years; the longer period applies to CEPA claims. [7][11]
