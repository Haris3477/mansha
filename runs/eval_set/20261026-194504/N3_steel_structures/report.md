# Mansha origin check: steel building structures

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The product qualifies for the preferential customs duty under the India-UAE CEPA because it meets the rule of origin. The exporter can claim the CEPA rate of 0%, which results in an estimated saving of 10000.00 AED. Please refer to the claim checklist in the report for the next steps.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 73089090, which covers other structures of iron or steel, with a high level of confidence. The exporter should confirm this tariff code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3][4][5]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| tariff 15%, effective 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate is 15% (with an effective rate of 10%), and the CEPA rate is 0%. The normal (MFN) rate is the duty charged when no trade-agreement preference is claimed. The CEPA rate applies only to Basic Customs Duty, so other import charges such as IGST may still be payable. The estimated saving on this shipment is 10000.00 AED. [1][2][3][4][5]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH) and at least 40% value addition (VA). CTSH means each non-originating input must be classified in a different 6-digit subheading than the final product, while VA is the share of the product's price that does not come from non-originating materials. The product meets this rule because the final manufacture was completed in the UAE, the work went beyond minimal operations, all non-originating inputs were classified outside subheading 730890, and the value addition reached 45.0% on an FOB basis, which is the price of the goods loaded on board at the UAE port. [6]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] Register the factory with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism through the Factory Registration service, which requires the factory to be located outside a free zone, a MoIAT industrial licence and a letter confirming 40% domestic value added; registration takes about three working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [7][8]
- [ ] Log in to the ministry e-services portal and choose the Certificate of Origin for India service.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [8][9][10][11]
- [ ] Fill in the application form with the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [12]
- [ ] Upload the required documents: proof of factory registration, the commercial invoice, and for a trading exporter the factory's purchase invoice.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [8][9][10][11]
- [ ] Pay the certificate fee online: AED 50 for an invoice value up to AED 100000.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [8][9][10][11]
- [ ] Submit the application and wait for approval, or a return with comments to correct; processing takes about one working day.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [8][9][10][11]
- [ ] Print the approved certificate.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [8][9][10][11]
- [ ] The certificate is valid for twelve months from the date of issue, covers only one import, and must be issued before, at or within five working days of export; it can be issued retrospectively up to twelve months from shipment with 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [12][13]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and must hold origin information under CAROTAR 2020, including which origin criterion is claimed.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [14][15][16]
- [ ] Keep origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least five years from the end of the calendar year of issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [12][13]

## Important notes

- The tariff schedule prints 15%, but an exemption notification lowers the duty actually charged to 10%; the effective rate is used. [2][3][4][5]
- Estimate on the shipment value given. India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. [2][3][4][5]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, SWS and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3][4][5]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3][4][5]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [6]
- '+' in a rule is read as 'and' (both tests must pass). The agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [6]
- Direct labour cost and direct overhead cost are not defined in the agreement. [6][12][13]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [7][8]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [8][9][11][12][13][17][18]
- Electronic certificates are reportedly accepted and uploaded to India's e-Sanchit system, but this is seen only in secondary sources. [14][15][16]
- The trade agreement does not specify whether the build-down and build-up methods use the same value-addition threshold, except for a 5-point adjustment when using Ex-Works pricing. [6][12][13]
