# Mansha origin check: aluminium window frames

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The product meets the rule of origin, so the CEPA rate can be claimed. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. See the checklist below for the required steps.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 76101000, Doors, windows and their frames and thresholds for doors (aluminium). Mansha's confidence in this classification is high, but you should confirm the code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| tariff 10%, effective 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal (MFN) rate is 10% and the CEPA rate is 0%. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin is CTSH plus VA 45%, meaning a change in tariff subheading and at least 45% value addition. CTSH requires each non‑originating input to be classified in a different 6‑digit subheading than the product, while VA is the share of the product's price that does not come from non‑originating materials. The product meets the rule because the final manufacture took place in the UAE, the work goes beyond minimal operations, all non‑originating inputs are classified outside subheading 761010, and the value addition is 49.0% on an FOB basis, exceeding the 45% threshold. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] Register the factory through the Ministry of Economy and Tourism's Factory Registration service (moet.gov.ae). Registration requires the factory to be located outside a free zone, a MoIAT industrial licence, and a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added. Processing takes about 3 working days.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] Apply for a Certificate of Origin for India through the e-service: log in to the ministry e-services portal, choose the Certificate of Origin for India service, fill in the application form, upload the required documents, pay the fee online, submit the application, and receive approval or a return with comments to correct.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] Pay the certificate fee. For an invoice value up to AED 100000, the fee is AED 50 (fee range: AED 10 to AED 200).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The certificate shall be issued prior to, at or within five working days of the date of exportation. It is valid for twelve months from the date of issue and covers only one import. A certificate may be issued retrospectively up to 12 months from the date of shipment, marked 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9 (Remarks).  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [10][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and must hold origin information under CAROTAR 2020, including which origin criterion is claimed. The importer keeps supporting documents for at least 5 years from filing and must answer a customs request within 10 working days.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter must keep origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least five years, starting from the end of the year of the date of issue of the certificate.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]

## Important notes

- Duty is estimated on the shipment value given. India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. [2][3]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, SWS and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The '+' symbol in the product-specific rule is read as 'and', meaning both the tariff shift and value addition tests must pass. The agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [4]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [4]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][9][10][11][15][16]
- Electronic certificates are reportedly accepted and uploaded to India's e-Sanchit system, but this is seen only in secondary sources (CBIC Instruction 28/2022 read through LiveLaw and taxmann), not the official text. [12][13][14]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [4][10][11]
