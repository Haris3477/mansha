# Mansha origin check: hardwood bedroom furniture

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The hardwood bedroom furniture meets the rule of origin, so the CEPA rate – the reduced duty under the India-UAE CEPA – can be claimed. This reduces the duty from the normal 20% to 12.5%, saving an estimated 7500.00 AED on the 100000 AED shipment. Caution: your factory is in a free zone, so it is likely not eligible for the factory registration needed before applying for the certificate of origin; confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism before relying on this result. See the checklist below for the steps to claim the CEPA rate.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 94036000, described as Other wooden furniture. Mansha’s confidence in this classification is high, but the code should be confirmed with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3][4]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| 20% (effective 20%) | 12.5% | 7500.00 AED |

The normal (MFN) rate, which is the duty charged when no trade‑agreement preference is claimed, is 20%, and the CEPA rate is 12.5% for this product. The estimated saving is 7500.00 AED on a shipment value of 100000 AED. [1][2][3][4]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff heading (CTH), meaning each non‑originating input must be classified in a different four‑digit heading than the product, and at least 40% value addition (VA), which is the share of the product’s price that does not come from non‑originating materials. The final manufacture check confirms that the last manufacturing step took place in the UAE, and the minimal operations check confirms that the work done goes beyond the simple operations that never give origin on their own. The CTH check passed because all 2 non‑originating inputs are classified outside heading 9403, and the VA check passed with a build‑down of 52.0% on an FOB basis, meaning the price loaded on board at the UAE port, exceeding the 40% threshold. Therefore the product meets the rule of origin. [5]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] Register your factory through the Ministry of Economy and Tourism's Factory Registration service (moet.gov.ae) before you apply for the certificate. The factory must be outside a free zone and hold a MoIAT industrial licence and a MoIAT letter confirming 40% domestic value added (a registration condition only, not your product's rule of origin). Registration takes about 3 working days. A trader can apply only for goods bought from a registered factory.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [6][7]  
  **Your factory is in a free zone: see the caution in the summary.**
- [ ] Apply through the 'Certificate of Origin for India' e-service (eservices.moec.gov.ae or the smart app): log in, choose the service, fill in the form, upload proof of factory registration and the commercial invoice (a trader also uploads the factory's purchase invoice), pay the fee online and submit. Processing takes about one working day. If the application is returned with comments, correct it; once approved, print the certificate.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] In the application, give the HS code, description, quantity and value of the product. Each item on the certificate must qualify separately. The issuing authority may ask for detailed cost sheets or visit the factory.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [11]
- [ ] Pay the certificate fee: AED 50 for an invoice value of AED 100,000 (fees run from AED 10 to AED 200 by invoice value).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [7][8][9][10]
- [ ] Have the certificate issued before, at, or within 5 working days of export. It is valid for 12 months from issue and covers one import (it can list several products).  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [11][12]
- [ ] Ship the goods directly from the UAE to India. Transit through another country is allowed only under customs control, with no operations there other than unloading, reloading, storage or similar. If a third party issues the invoice, indicate 'third-party invoicing'.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [11][12]
- [ ] Your Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry, and the importer or their customs broker uploads the certificate to e-Sanchit. Under CAROTAR 2020 the importer must hold origin information, including which origin criterion is claimed, and still has a duty of reasonable care, so you may be asked for production and cost details.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [13][14][15]
- [ ] Keep your origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least 5 years from the end of the calendar year in which the certificate was issued. India may ask the UAE authority to verify the certificate, and refusing a verification visit is grounds for denying the preference.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [11][12]

## Important notes

- The '+' in the rule is read as 'and' (both tests must pass); the agreement does not define '+', so this is Mansha's interpretation. [5]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [5]
- A factory declared in a free zone is likely not eligible for the ministry's factory registration; confirm with the ministry. [6][7]
- The duty estimate is based on the shipment value given; India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. [2][3][4]
- The CEPA rate reduces Basic Customs Duty only; IGST, GST compensation cess, the Social Welfare Surcharge and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3][4]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3][4]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement; the agreement does not say whether the build-down and build-up methods use the same threshold apart from the 5-point Ex-Works adjustment. [5][11][12]
- By interpretation, de minimis helps only the tariff-shift part of a rule, not the VA percentage. [11][12]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [7][8][10][11][12][16][17]
- The Social Welfare Surcharge is likely charged on the reduced BCD (no CEPA-specific text on it was found); anti-dumping and safeguard duties likely still apply. [15][18]
- The CEPA reduces the duty on this line but does not eliminate it. [1][2][3][4]
- UAE law has a 3‑year minimum, but the CEPA’s 5‑year rule applies to CEPA claims. [11][12]
