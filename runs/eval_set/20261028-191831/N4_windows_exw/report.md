# Mansha origin check: aluminium windows

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

The aluminium windows meet the rule of origin, so the CEPA rate can be claimed. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. See the checklist below for the steps to claim the CEPA rate.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 76101000, described as doors, windows and their frames and thresholds for doors (aluminium). Mansha's confidence in this classification is medium, and the code should be confirmed with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 0% | 10000.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal MFN rate is 10% and the CEPA rate is 0%. The CEPA rate applies only to Basic Customs Duty, so other import charges such as IGST are still payable. The estimated saving is 10000.00 AED. [1][2][3]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff subheading (CTSH) and value addition (VA) of at least 45%, where CTSH means each non‑originating input must be classified in a different 6‑digit subheading than the product and VA is the share of the price that does not come from non‑originating materials. The final manufacture took place in the UAE and the work went beyond minimal operations, and all three non‑originating inputs are classified outside subheading 761010, so CTSH is satisfied. The value addition is 43% on an Ex‑Works basis, which meets the 40% threshold after the 5‑point adjustment, so VA is also satisfied, meaning the product meets the rule of origin. [4]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] The Ministry of Economy and Tourism registers the factory (prerequisite) before applying for a certificate of origin for India.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [5][6]
- [ ] The exporter applies for a certificate of origin for India through the e-service: logs in to the ministry e-services portal, chooses the Certificate of Origin for India service, fills in the application form, uploads the required documents, submits the application, and receives approval or a return with comments to correct, then prints the approved certificate.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The Ministry of Foreign Trade charges a certificate fee of AED 50 (band: invoice value up to AED 100000).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [6][7][8][9]
- [ ] The competent authority issues the certificate of origin, which is valid for 12 months from the date of issue, covers only one import, and should be issued prior to, at, or within five working days of the date of exportation; it may be issued retrospectively up to 12 months from the date of shipment, marked 'ISSUED RETROSPECTIVELY' in box 9 (Remarks).  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [10][11]
- [ ] The issuing authority requires the application to include the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product, and each item must qualify separately in its own right; it may apply risk management, including asking for detailed cost sheets and conducting a factory visit.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [10]
- [ ] The exporter ensures that the goods are shipped directly from the UAE to India; transit through another country is allowed only under customs control, with no operations other than unloading, reloading, storage or similar; if the invoice is issued by a third party, the exporter must indicate 'third-party invoicing'.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry, holds origin information under CAROTAR 2020 including which origin criterion is claimed, keeps supporting documents for at least five years from filing, and must answer a customs request within ten working days.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [12][13][14]
- [ ] The exporter keeps origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least five years starting from the end of the year of the date of its issue; any failure to consent to a verification visit shall be grounds for a denial of preferential benefits.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [10][11]

## Important notes

- The operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [4]
- The duty estimate is on the shipment value given; India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ. Shown in the shipment's own currency; never converted. [2][3]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, the Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3]
- Direct labour cost and direct overhead cost are not defined in the agreement. [4][10][11]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [5][6]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [6][7][9][10][11][15][16]
- The Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) is likely charged on the reduced BCD (no CEPA-specific text on it was found). [14][17]
- Anti-dumping and safeguard duties likely still apply (no CEPA exemption from them was found). [14][17]
- The 8-digit code 76101090 you gave is not a line in India's tariff; Mansha matched it on its first 6 digits (761010). 8-digit codes are national and differ between the UAE and India. [1][2][3]
- The plus sign in the rule 'CTSH + VA 45%' is read as 'and', meaning both the CTSH and VA tests must be satisfied. [4]
- For CEPA claims, the required record‑keeping period is 5 years, even though UAE law sets a minimum of 3 years. [10][11]
