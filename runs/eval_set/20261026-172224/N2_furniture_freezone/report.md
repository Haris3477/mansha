# Mansha origin check: hardwood bedroom furniture

**Result: Eligible for the CEPA rate**

## Summary

This product is eligible for the preferential duty rate under the India-UAE CEPA because it meets the rule of origin. By claiming the CEPA rate of 12.5% instead of the normal rate of 20%, you can save an estimated 7500.00 AED on your shipment. Please follow the checklist below to complete the claim process.

## Product classification

The product is classified under tariff line 94036000, which is described as Other wooden furniture. Mansha has high confidence in this classification, but you should still confirm the code with Indian customs or a customs broker before shipping. [1][2][3][4]

## Duty comparison

| Normal (MFN) rate | CEPA rate | Estimated saving |
|---|---|---|
| tariff 20%, effective 20% | 12.5% | 7500.00 AED |

Under the India-UAE CEPA, the normal import duty rate (MFN rate) is 20%, and the reduced CEPA rate is 12.5%. By claiming the CEPA rate, you can save an estimated 7500.00 AED on this shipment. [1][2][3][4]

## Rule of origin and result

The rule of origin requires a change in tariff heading (CTH) and a value addition (VA) of at least 40% on an FOB basis. CTH means that each non-originating input must be classified in a different 4-digit heading than the final product, and VA is the share of the product's price that does not come from non-originating materials. The product meets the rule because the final manufacture took place in the UAE, and the work goes beyond minimal operations. Additionally, all 2 non-originating inputs are classified outside heading 9403, and the value addition of 52.0% is above the 40% threshold. [5]

## Step-by-step checklist to claim the CEPA rate

- [ ] Register the factory with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism through its Factory Registration service; registration requires the factory to be outside a free zone, an industrial licence and a letter confirming 40% domestic value added.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Economy and Tourism.* [6][7]
- [ ] Include in the application the HS code, description, quantity and value of the exported product; each item must qualify separately.  
  *Authority: The issuing authority.* [8]
- [ ] Apply through the 'Certificate of Origin for India' e-service: log in to the ministry e-services portal, choose the service, fill in the application form, upload the required documents, pay the fee online, submit the application and print the approved certificate.  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [7][9][10][11]
- [ ] Pay the certificate fee, which is estimated at AED 50 for an invoice value up to AED 100,000 (the fee range is AED 10 to AED 200).  
  *Authority: The Ministry of Foreign Trade.* [7][9][10][11]
- [ ] The certificate must be issued before, at, or within five working days of export; it is valid for twelve months from issue and covers only one import.  
  *Authority: The competent authority.* [8][12]
- [ ] Ship the goods directly from the UAE to India; if a third party issues the invoice, the exporter must indicate 'third-party invoicing'.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [8][12]
- [ ] The Indian importer claims the CEPA rate when filing the bill of entry and must hold origin information, including which origin criterion is claimed.  
  *Authority: The Indian importer.* [13][14][15]
- [ ] Keep origin records, including the cost breakdown, for at least five years from the end of the calendar year of issue.  
  *Authority: The exporter.* [8][12]

## Important notes

- The agreement does not define '+' in the rule; Mansha interprets it as 'and' (both tests must pass). [5]
- Operations and final manufacture are the exporter's own declaration. [5]
- India charges duty on the assessable (CIF) value at import, so actual amounts will differ from the estimate. [2][3][4]
- CEPA rates reduce Basic Customs Duty (BCD) only. IGST, GST compensation cess, SWS and any anti-dumping or safeguard duty still apply. [2][3][4]
- The CEPA rate applies only if the product qualifies under the rules of origin and a valid certificate of origin is presented. [2][3][4]
- 'Direct labour cost' and 'direct overhead cost' are not defined in the agreement. [5][8][12]
- Interpretation: de minimis helps only the tariff-shift part of a rule, not the VA percentage. [8][12]
- No official page read names the Ministry of Foreign Trade as the issuer of the India CEPA certificate specifically: its service page covers preferential certificates in general, and the India-specific page is still on moet.gov.ae. [7][8][9][11][12][16][17]
- A producer in a free zone is likely not eligible for Factory Registration, because it requires a factory outside a free zone, and registration is needed before applying for the certificate of origin for India. Confirm with the Ministry of Economy and Tourism. [6][7]
- Inference: SWS is charged on the reduced BCD (no CEPA-specific text found). [15][18]
- Inference: anti-dumping and safeguard duties still apply (no exemption found). [15][18]
- Electronic certificates are reportedly accepted and uploaded to India's e-Sanchit system, but this is seen only in secondary sources (CBIC Instruction 28/2022 read through LiveLaw, taxmann), not the official text. [13][14][15]
- Be aware that while the agreement mentions box 8 for the certificate, India's origin rules and certificate form require box 9. Since India's form governs entry into the country, you should use box 9 on your certificate. [8][12]
- If your invoice is issued by a third party, this does not automatically deny your claim. However, you must clearly indicate 'third-party invoicing' on your documentation. [8][12]
